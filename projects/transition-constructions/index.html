glow up and throwback constructions

How do sound anchors determine the content and layout of social media artefacts?

At the moment, this project encompasses one of two branches of an analysis of multimodal social media constructions. In this branch, I investigate the use of audio as a determing factor in the form and meaning of transition constructions, used to share “glow ups” and “throwbacks”. In the other branch, I explore how social media users use trending audio templates to show off their pets. Ultimately, I believe in an expanded, multimodal definition of construction, and I believe that such a definition can be helpful in studying social media discourse and in-group dynamics.

Building on the literature on both multimodal and discourse constructions, I argue that 1) individual “glow up” and “throwback” trends constitute micro-level multimodal constructions that group together to form a more abstract transition construction, and 2) audio plays a prominent role in determining the form of the constructions, at all levels of abstraction. The corpus for this project contains thirty multimodal artefacts that can be classified as part of the “Subway” trend (either Instagram Reels or TikToks) as well as select examples from six other transition trends.

Here’s an instance of a creator participating in the trend to showcase her move to Gothenburg.

View this post on Instagram

A post shared by CAJSA (KY-suh) (@cajsathecreator)

The narrative structure of the video hinges on the transitional decision of leaving one location in order to move to another (Gothenburg) and draws on the metonymic relationship between someplace and living someplace to depict events in the creator’s life. The video accompanying the first half of the audio clip, “I made a promise if in four months / this feeling ain’t gone,” shows the creator wiping away tears, while crying in a car, an undisputable performance of unhappiness. The second shot, taken from inside an airplane, signals the transitional moment of being in between places and accompanies the verse, “Well, fuck this city.” The third shot, which accompanies, “I’m moving to Saskatchewan,” depicts the creator as happy and carefree, smiling while riding a motorcycle in their new place of living. In addition to the auditory and visual layers, there is also a third layer that needs to be looked at: the linguistic component of the caption. In the first shot, the caption mirrors the segment of the audio clip it co-occurs with; in the second shot, the caption censors the word fuck as f*ck but is otherwise identical to the audio segment; in the third shot, the caption replaces Saskatchewan with Göteborg. These three components come together to constitute the form of this construct.

Here’s a video that uses two slots to showcase before and after a creator embraces her sexuality.

View this post on Instagram

A post shared by Drew Jarvis (@drewrjarvis)

While the first video corresponded to three slots—before, transitional, and after, the transitional slot is optional and can be merged into either the before or the after slot. This last video shows a construct where the transitional slot has attached to the after slot. The linguistic components in both slots have been adapted to explain the situation, and so the Reel is quite explicit about what has been left (doubts about her sexuality) and what she has moved to (post-divorce life and embracing her sexuality) .

Now, in this video below, a creator shares the outcome of her decision to leave her career as a surgeon.

View this post on Instagram

A post shared by Diana Montalvo | Life Outside Medicine (@dianamontalvostudio)

This video constitutes another prototypical example, where the creator is sharing about leaving her job as a surgeon. The first slot contains a video from the creator’s past, before the pivotal decision was made, in which she is wearing scrubs, which represent her career as a surgeon. The caption of the first slot corresponds exactly to the segment of the song that it accompanies. The second slot, corresponding to the audio segment, “fuck this city,” contains a video of the creator in a transitional state, in the car, most probably behind the steering wheel; the caption is broken into three separate episodes, well, f, and being a surgeon. The third slot, then, retains the audio segment but contains a more creative linguistic adaptation of “I’m moving to Saskatchewan”: “I’m moving to a different career path.” The visual component of the third slot shows the creator, smiling, presumably happy about her decision to leave her career as a surgeon.

Finally, as a treat, enjoy this funny Reel that uses the form of the trend to humorously praise the 5A bus route in Dundee:

View this post on Instagram

A post shared by 5/5A Fanpage (@5.5a.fanpage)

In this Reel, posted by a fan page for bus route 5A in Dundee, Scotland, the creator is not visible in the videos, instead the first and the third slot contain pictures of regional transit (perhaps, as stand-ins for the regions themselves), and the transitional slot contains a typical visual of an airplane window view. Despite the lack of facial expressions and other gestures of joy, the meaning imposed by the form of the construction is that Dundee has better public transportation, than the region shown in the first slot.
