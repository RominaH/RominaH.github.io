This is my master's thesis. Looking at linguistic data from both online and on-the-ground social movements, I use Cognitive Construction Grammar as a framework to explore how protest slogans are formed and how they can be borrowed (or co-opted) by parties outside of their original counterpublics. I take three distinct avenues of investigation, drawing on rhetoric, corpus linguistics, and psycholinguistics. The research questions investigated are 1) what rhetorical figures impact where a slogan construction can be borrowed/co-opted, 2) how can exemplar representations help describe the semantic and syntactic features that influence whether a construction can be taken outside of the community in which it originated, and 3) does the relative frequency of tokens in schematic slogan constructs impact reading time?
