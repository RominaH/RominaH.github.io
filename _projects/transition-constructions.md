At the moment, this project encompasses one of two branches of an analysis of multimodal social media constructions. In this branch, I investigate the use of audio as a determing factor in the form and meaning of transition constructions, used to share "glow ups" and "throwbacks". In the other branch, I explore how social media users use trending audio templates to show off their pets. Ultimately, I believe in an expanded, multimodal definition of construction, and I believe that such a definition can be helpful in studying social media discourse and in-group dynamics.

Building on the literature on both multimodal and discourse constructions, I argue that 1) individual “glow up” and “throwback” trends constitute micro-level multimodal constructions that group together to form a more 
abstract transition construction, and 2) audio plays a prominent role in determining the form of the constructions, at all levels of abstraction. The corpus for this project contains thirty 
multimodal artefacts that can be classified as part of the “Subway” trend (either Instagram Reels or TikToks) as well as select examples from six other transition trends.

The narrative structure of the video hinges on the transitional decision of leaving one location in order to move to another (Gothenburg) and draws on the metonymic relationship between someplace and living someplace to depict events in the creator’s life. The video accompanying the first half of the audio clip, “I made a promise if in four months / this feeling ain’t gone,” shows the creator wiping away tears, while crying in a car, an undisputable performance of unhappiness. The second shot, taken from inside an airplane, signals the transitional moment of being in between places and accompanies the verse, “Well, fuck this city.” The third shot, which accompanies, “I’m moving to Saskatchewan,” depicts the creator as happy and carefree, smiling while riding a motorcycle in their new place of living. In addition to the auditory and visual layers, there is also a third layer that needs to be looked at: the linguistic component of the caption. In the first shot, the caption mirrors the segment of the audio clip it co-occurs with; in the second shot, the caption censors the word _fuck_ as _f*ck_ but is otherwise identical to the audio segment; in the third shot, the caption replaces _Saskatchewan_ with _Göteborg_. These three components come together to constitute the form of this construct.

While the first video corresponded to three slots—_before_, _transitional_, and _after_, the _transitional_ slot is optional and can be merged into either the _before_ or the _after_ slot. This last video shows a construct where the _transitional_ slot has attached to the _after_ slot. The linguistic components in both slots have been adapted to explain the situation, and so the Reel is quite explicit about what has been left (doubts about her sexuality) and what she has moved to (post-divorce life and embracing her sexuality).
